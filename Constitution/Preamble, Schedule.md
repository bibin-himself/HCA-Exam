`///// PREAMBLE \\\\\`
 Introduction to the Constitution
 Describes `Philosophy`, `Objectives` & `Ideals` of Contitution
 `Soul`; `Identity Card` of the Constitution  - N.A. Palkhivala

 Opening Words : WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA
   : `Source of authority of the constitution`
     - Derives its power from the people of India

 Nature of the Indian State:
  Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic

 `4 Objectives`
   <Justice>     : Social, Economic & Political
   <Liberty>     : Thought, Expression, Belief, Faith & Worship
   <Equality>    : Status & Oppurtunity
   <Fraternity>  : Dignity of the individual & Unity and Integrity of the Nation (Common brotherhood)

  Adopted on : 26 Nov 1949


  Sovereign   –  Independent
  Socialist   –  Social and economic justice, Mixed economy
  Secular     –  Equal respect for religions, Positive secularism
  Democratic  –  Elected government, Indirect democracy
  Republic    –  Elected Head of State







`///// 42nd Amendment Act 1976  \\\\\`
  Changes in Preamble
  AKA Mini Constitution
  Words inserted:
   Socialist, Secular, Integrity (in Unity & Integrity)

  * “Sovereign Democratic Republic”  →  “Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic”
  * “Unity of the Nation”            →  “Unity and Integrity of the Nation”





`///// Important Cases Related  \\\\\`

> 1. Berubari Union Case – 1960
  Held that the `Preamble is not part` of the Constitution.
  This position was later reconsidered.

> 2. Kesavananda Bharati Case – 1973
  Held that the `Preamble is part` of the Constitution.
  Preamble can be `amended under Article 368`
  Amendment `cannot destroy the Basic Structure` of the Constitution.

> 3. LIC of India v. Union of India – 1995
  `Reaffirmed` the `importance of the Preamble` as an integral part of the Constitution.











`///// UNION & IT'S TERRITORY (PART 1)  \\\\\`
 
 > Article 1 – `Name and Territory of the Union`
    “India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States.”
    India is described as a Union of States 

 > Article 2 - `Admission or Establishment of New States`
    Parliament can admit new States into the Union.

 > Article 3 - `Formation and Alteration of Existing States`
    Parliament has the power to:
     - Form a new State by separating territory from any State.
     - unite two or more States or parts of States.
     - Increase or Decrease the area of any State
     - Alter the boundaries or name of any State

    `/// Conditions under ARTICLE 3 ///`
      1. `Prior recommendation of the President` is required to introduce the Bill.
      2. President refers it to the `concerned State Legislature` for its `views`.
      3. State's `consent is not binding` on Parliament.
    



`/// ARTICLE 4 \\\`
  – Supplemental, Incidental and Consequential Provisions

   Laws made under Articles 2 and 3:
   Are `not` treated as `Constitutional Amendments` under Article 368.
   Can be `passed by` a `simple majority` in Parliament.
